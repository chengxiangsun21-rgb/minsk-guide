MINSK · BELARUS

Minsk City Guide

The capital of Belarus · A green city on the Svislach River

Minsk is the capital and largest city of Belarus, home to nearly two million people on the banks of the Svislach River. Wide Stalinist avenues, Soviet-era metro stations, post-war memorial squares and old churches tucked into the lanes of the Upper Town give the city its unusual character. Two or three days is enough to see the best of it.

Top Sights

City landmark

Independence Square & the Red Church

Ploshcha Niezalezhnastsi

One of the largest squares in Europe, flanked by the House of Government from the early 1930s and the main building of Belarusian State University. On its western corner stands the Church of Saints Simon and Helena, built of dark red brick in 1905–1910 — the most photographed building in Minsk.

🚇 Metro Line 1 · Ploshcha Lenina station
Memorial

Victory Square

Ploshcha Peramohi

Minsk's main WWII memorial: a tall granite obelisk crowned with the Order of Victory, an eternal flame burning at its foot, and an underground hall of memory whose walls carry the names of decorated soldiers and partisans.

🚇 Metro Line 1 · Ploshcha Peramohi station
Memorial

Island of Tears

Ostrov Slyoz

A small artificial island in the Svislach, reached by a footbridge from Trinity Suburb. Its memorial complex honours the Belarusian soldiers who died in the Soviet–Afghan War of 1979–1989; the weeping angel statue is the island's most moving sight.

🚇 On foot: footbridge from Trinity Suburb
Architectural wonder

National Library of Belarus

Natsionalnaya Biblioteka

A glass rhombicuboctahedron opened in 2006, rising more than twenty storeys high and changing colour after dark — Minsk's most futuristic landmark. The top-floor observation deck gives a panoramic view over the whole city.

🚇 Metro Line 1 · Uschod station
Old town walk

Upper Town & Freedom Square

Verkhni Gorod

The closest Minsk comes to an old town: the medieval market square, the city hall, and the blue-and-white Holy Spirit Cathedral, part of a 17th-century monastery. One of the few pockets of pre-war Minsk still standing — best wandered at dusk.

🚇 Metro Line 2 · Nyamiha station
City landmark

Minsk Gate

Minskaya Brama

Twin eleven-storey towers in Stalinist Empire style, completed in 1953 to greet every traveller arriving by train. One tower carries the largest clock in Belarus — many visitors take their first Minsk photo right here.

🚇 Metro Line 1 · Ploshcha Lenina station

What to Eat

Belarusian food is hearty and warming — don't miss these four.

Draniki (Potato Pancakes)

The national dish: grated potato fried until golden and crisp outside, soft inside. Always with a spoonful of sour cream.

Borscht

A deep-red beetroot soup served with sour cream and dark bread — the warmest thing you can eat on a cold day.

Kvass

A lightly sweet, slightly fizzy drink fermented from dark bread. Sold on street corners in summer for a couple of rubles a cup.

Kolduny (Belarusian Dumplings)

Hearty meat-filled dumplings served with butter and sour cream. Made for the end of a long day of walking.

Good to Know

A quick read before you go saves a few wrong turns.

  • The metro has 3 lines covering the centre and is cheap; Line 1 runs under Independence Avenue, which makes it the easiest way to explore the main axis.
  • The currency is the Belarusian ruble (BYN). Cards are widely accepted in malls, but keep some cash for small shops and museum tickets.
  • Plugs are type C / F, 220V — bring an adapter if your devices use a different plug.
  • May to September is the best time for walking; winters are long and cold, so dress warmly.
  • Russian is the everyday language; younger people speak some English. A couple of Russian phrases for directions go a long way.
